> Last week was Science and Environment Week at Atatürk Anatolian High School. On Monday, the science club gave a short lecture about recycling, and every class had to prepare a poster. **B2. (Orta, ENG.8.4.R3)** Read the text and answer the questions.

> **Two Homes, One Family**
> I'm Berk, and I live in a flat on the third floor in Bursa with my parents and my little sister. Our flat is small but cosy. Every summer we spend a month at my grandparents' house in a village near İznik Lake. Their house is a traditional stone house with a big garden and a wooden balcony. There is no lift and no dishwasher, but there is always fresh bread from the oven. My grandmother says a house is not about technology; it is about the people in it. In the village, my sister and I look after the chickens and help in the garden. In Bursa, we only help with the dishes. I love both homes, but I feel freer in the village.

1. Who does Berk live with in Bursa?
2. Where is his grandparents' house?
3. What does Berk do in the village that he doesn't do in Bursa?
4. What does the grandmother think about houses?
5. T / F / NM: a) Berk's flat is on the fifth floor. b) The village house has a wooden balcony. c) Berk's grandparents are farmers.
6. What does "it" refer to in "it is about the people in it"?

**B3. (Zor, ENG.8.4.R3)** Read the message and answer the questions.

What should I do?

3. Why doesn't Ela's mother listen to her as much as before?

**C3. (Orta, ENG.8.8.R3)** Read the text and answer the questions.

> **Flying to School?**
> In 2045, many students will not take the bus to school. Scientists are designing small electric air-taxis that can carry six passengers. The vehicles will use batteries, so they won't produce dirty air. A trip from home to school will take only five minutes, and there will be no traffic jams in the sky. However, air-taxis will need special landing areas on school roofs, and they might be too expensive for small towns. If engineers solve the battery problem, the first flying school buses will start in big cities. Some parents are worried. "If something goes wrong in the air, what will happen?" asks a mother from İzmir. Engineers answer that the vehicles will have four backup motors and will land safely.

4. What will happen if engineers solve the battery problem?
5. T / F? Correct the false one(s): a) The trip to school will take fifteen minutes. b) The vehicles will have backup motors.
6. Complete: *If something goes wrong in the air, the vehicle ________ (land) safely.*

**C4. (Zor, ENG.8.8.R3)** Read the text and answer the questions.

> **Who Is Driving?**
> Self-driving cars are not science fiction any more. They use cameras, sensors and artificial intelligence to see the road. Supporters say they will reduce accidents because machines don't get tired or use phones while driving. Old people and people who can't drive will travel more freely, and cities will need fewer car parks because the cars will share rides. On the other hand, critics say hackers might take control of a car, and many taxi and bus drivers will lose their jobs. There is another question: if a self-driving car has an accident, who is responsible, the passenger or the company? In my opinion, we will see these cars everywhere in twenty years, but governments must write new laws first.

1. Complete the table with **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of self-driving cars from the text.
2. Why will self-driving cars have fewer accidents, according to supporters?
3. What does "they" refer to in "they will reduce accidents"?
4. What is the writer's opinion?
5. Inference: Is the writer completely against self-driving cars? Explain with evidence from the text.
